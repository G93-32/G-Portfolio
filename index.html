THE DEVELOPER

Curiosity. Patience. Stubbornness. A refusal to treat any tool as a black box. These are the things that turn someone who uses software into someone who builds it.

I'm Gurjot Singh — a developer who learns by building the thing rather than reading about it. My work runs from hand-written, SEO-optimised websites to machine-learning algorithms implemented from the mathematics up.

No frameworks where the platform will do. No copied snippets I couldn't rewrite from memory. The goal is always the same: understand it well enough to build it twice.

BEGINNINGS

It started the way it starts for most people who stick with it — a blank file, a compiler error, and the specific irritation of not understanding why.

First C, then Python. Loops before libraries, pointers before packages. That order matters: it's the reason a framework feels like a convenience rather than a mystery.

hello.c
/* where it started */
#include <stdio.h>

int main(void) {
    printf("hello, world\n");
    return 0;
